## Overview

Phantom armies (also called spectral armies, ghost armies, or, in the Cretan tradition, Drosoulites - 'dew shadows') are reports of mass apparitions involving multiple soldiers, cavalry, or whole armies, typically associated with historical battlefields and sometimes with sites of large-scale military death. The category is distinct from individual battlefield ghosts in that the apparitions are explicitly collective and often re-enact organised military formations, marches, or combat. Famous cases include the 1642-43 phantom re-enactment of the Battle of Edgehill in Warwickshire, England (the first major battle of the English Civil War), described in the contemporary pamphlet 'A Great Wonder in Heaven' (1643); the Drosoulites of Frangokastello in Crete, an annual late-May/early-June apparition associated with the 1828 Battle of Frangokastello during the Greek War of Independence; and the long-standing tradition of phantom soldiers and ghostly engagements at Gettysburg, Pennsylvania, site of the bloodiest battle of the American Civil War (1-3 July 1863). Skeptical scientific framings explain at least some cases as mirages or Fata Morgana - particularly the Drosoulites - as well as misidentification, suggestion, and pareidolia. The category does not include the World-War-II 23rd Headquarters Special Troops ('Ghost Army'), an Allied deception unit and a separate phenomenon.

## Etymology

- **Origin**: English compound from 'phantom' (Greek phantasma, 'apparition') and 'army'.
- **Meaning**: Apparitional collectives of soldiers - whether marching, fighting, or simply present - reported at battlefields and other military sites.
- **First use**: The English compound is recorded in 19th-century paranormal and folkloric writing; underlying traditions of apparitional armies are documented from antiquity (e.g. Plutarch on the apparitions at Marathon).

## Notable Sightings

### Phantom re-enactment of the Battle of Edgehill ⭐
> **Date**: 1642-12-24  
> **Location**: Edge Hill, Warwickshire, England, near Kineton  
> **Witnesses**: 0 (Local shepherds and townspeople, Royal Commission appointed by Charles I)  
Around Christmas 1642, shepherds and travellers near Edge Hill reported hearing the sounds of battle and seeing a ghostly re-enactment of the engagement of 23 October 1642 in the night sky, including cavalry charges and the clash of arms. The pamphlet A Great Wonder in Heaven (published 23 January 1643) records the event; King Charles I subsequently sent a Royal Commission, which is said to have observed the apparitions and identified specific officers, including the king's standard-bearer Sir Edmund Verney.
**Credibility**: medium — One of the best-documented early-modern phantom-army reports.
**Sources**: A Great Wonder in Heaven (London, 1643)

---

### Drosoulites of Frangokastello ⭐
> **Date**: 1828-05-17  
> **Location**: Frangokastello, Sfakia, southern coast of Crete  
> **Witnesses**: 0 (Local Cretan residents and visitors over many years)  
Around the anniversary of the Battle of Frangokastello (17 May 1828), residents of the area report seeing a procession of dark, human-like shadows moving from the monastery of Agios Charalambos toward the Frangokastello fortress, dissolving when they reach the sea. The phenomenon, called Drosoulites ('dew shadows') by locals, typically lasts about ten minutes, occurring at dawn under calm and humid conditions. Mainstream scientific explanation links the phenomenon to a Fata Morgana superior mirage of objects on the coast of North Africa.
**Credibility**: medium — Among the best-documented modern collective apparition phenomena, with a strong atmospheric-optics counter-explanation.
**Sources**: Wikipedia: Drosoulites

---

### Battlefield apparitions at Gettysburg
> **Date**: 1863-07-01  
> **Location**: Gettysburg battlefield, Adams County, Pennsylvania  
> **Witnesses**: 0 (Visitors, residents, park staff over many years)  
Since the late 19th century, visitors, locals, and park staff have reported phantom soldiers, the sounds of musketry and cannon, and at times groups or formations of period-uniformed figures across various sectors of the Gettysburg battlefield, including Devil's Den, Little Round Top, and the Triangular Field.
**Credibility**: low — Best-known American battlefield apparition tradition.
**Sources**: Mark Nesbitt, Ghosts of Gettysburg series

## Theories & Explanations

### Paranormal / Cryptozoological

**Residual / 'stone tape' haunting** _(20th-century paranormal investigators (e.g. T. C. Lethbridge, Peter Underwood))_

Within paranormal frameworks, phantom armies are typically explained as residual hauntings - environmental recordings of intense events 'replayed' under suitable conditions, with no consciousness involved.

**Restless mass dead** _(Traditional folkloric explanation)_

Folkloric explanation: those who died violently in battle remain unsettled and re-enact their last moments.

### Scientific

**Fata Morgana / superior mirage** _(Greek meteorologists and paranormal researchers)_

At least the Drosoulites have been credibly explained as Fata Morgana superior mirages, in which objects (potentially on the North African coast) are refracted into apparently animated images visible across the sea.

### Skeptical

**Suggestion, expectation, and selective memory** _(Joe Nickell, Benjamin Radford)_

Skeptical analysts argue phantom-army experiences arise from a combination of suggestion (visiting a famous battlefield primes apparition reports), pareidolia in low light, and selective recall.

**Pamphlet / propaganda explanation (Edgehill)** _(English Civil War historians)_

Some historians argue the 1643 Edgehill pamphlet was Civil War-era political propaganda rather than a straightforward report.
